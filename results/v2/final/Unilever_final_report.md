### Governance Oversight and Accountability

Unilever’s Board holds ultimate accountability for the management of all material impacts, risks, and opportunities (IROs), including sustainability-related matters, and delegates day-to-day oversight to the Unilever Leadership Executive (ULE) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. The Board’s governance structure is supported by four key subcommittees: the Corporate Responsibility Committee reviews the sustainability strategy and progress against goals five times per year; the Audit Committee oversees risk management—including the double materiality assessment—and assurance over non-financial disclosures; the Remuneration Committee aligns the long-term Performance Share Plan (PSP) with sustainability performance; and the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee ensures Board composition includes relevant sustainability expertise [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. In 2024, the ULE approved a refocused sustainability strategy with 15 externally facing goals across climate, nature, plastics, and livelihoods—each assigned to a ULE member with clear mitigation plans and timelines [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_8]. To strengthen execution, Unilever re-established an Executive-level Sustainability Steering Committee in 2025, meeting monthly to review progress and resolve delivery issues [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_8].

### Anti-Corruption and Business Integrity Framework

Unilever maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward corruption and bribery, as codified in its Code of Business Principles (COBP) and supporting Code Policies, which prohibit public and commercial bribery—including facilitation payments—regardless of financial value [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_164]. These policies apply to all employees and extend to business partners via the Responsible Partner Policy (RPP), which mandates adherence to Unilever’s anti-corruption standards across Tier 1 suppliers and cascades expectations into their own supply chains [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_164][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_161]. Prevention is reinforced through annual mandatory anti-corruption and anti-bribery training for all employees and Board members, sponsored by the Chief Legal Officer and Chief Business Integrity Officer and overseen by the Corporate Responsibility Committee [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_165]. Periodic bespoke risk assessments identify high-risk geographies and business activities to inform targeted controls and tailored training [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_165][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_164]. Detection and response mechanisms include confidential reporting channels, centralised investigation protocols led by the Global Head of Investigations, and sanctions determined by the Global Code and Policy Committee—with oversight from the Chief Legal Officer and Chief Business Integrity Officer for cases involving public officials or senior executives [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_161]. ### Integration of Sustainability into Risk Management and Incentives

Sustainability governance is embedded within Unilever’s enterprise risk management framework. The double materiality assessment (DMA) informs the identification and annual formal review of principal risks—including Legal and Compliance risk—and integrates inputs such as global fraud risk assessments and geopolitical screening to proactively address corruption and bribery exposure [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_156]. Sustainability performance is formally linked to executive remuneration: the long-term PSP award incorporates the Sustainability Progress Index (SPI), which accounts for 15% of the total award and assesses performance against four 2025 targets covering climate, nature, plastics, and livelihoods [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_8][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_9]. The SPI is jointly assessed by the Corporate Responsibility and Remuneration Committees, and ULE and Board progress is reviewed quarterly [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_9]. Sustainability due diligence is governed through a structured process with ULE Sponsors appointed for each ESRS topic, alongside designated Sustainability and Business owners responsible for narrative and metric disclosures, under the oversight of the Group Controller to ensure data integrity [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_9].
