### GRI 403 Occupational Health & Safety  
Apple’s Environment, Health and Safety (EHS) Policy states its commitment to protecting the health and safety of employees, customers, and contractors across all global operations, including design, research, manufacturing, distribution, and product use [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_201]. The policy mandates that all employees receive EHS training in their primary language and requires suppliers to provide safe working conditions and treat workers with dignity and respect [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_201]. Apple applies higher EHS standards where local laws are inadequate and integrates best practices into all business aspects to conserve resources for future generations [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_201]. Its EHS management systems are certified to ISO 14001:2015 for Apple Operations International Limited in Cork, Ireland, covering EMEA operational activities including manufacturing, sales, delivery, and after-sales support; this certification was issued on 11 July 2024 and remains valid until 10 July 2027 [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_202].

### Governance Structure and Oversight

Apple’s governance framework integrates environmental, social, and governance (ESG) responsibilities into its core management accountability. Management is explicitly responsible for assertions related to the allocation of green bond proceeds—including selection of Eligibility Criteria and verification of project alignment—under the Apple Green Bond framework [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_160]. This responsibility is affirmed in management’s formal assertion covering the period from September 29, 2019, to September 28, 2024, stating that $1.1 billion in net proceeds from the 0.000% notes due 2025 and 0.500% notes due 2031 were allocated to qualifying Eligible Projects [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_159]. Independent assurance on this assertion was provided by an external firm engaged annually to assess conformance with the Use of Proceeds criteria and reporting commitments [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_160].

Apple embeds climate governance across strategic decision-making through formal advocacy principles and cross-sectoral partnerships. These principles inform participation in over ten multi-stakeholder initiatives—including the Responsible Business Alliance (RBA), World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD), and Climate Group’s RE100—where Apple serves on governing bodies such as the RBA Board of Directors and the MIT Climate & Sustainability Consortium Industry Advisory Board [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_107][Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_106].
