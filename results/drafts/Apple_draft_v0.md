## Environmental

### Environmental Performance Report: Apple Inc.  
*Based on the 2025 Environmental Progress Report and verified disclosures*

#### ### GRI 302 Energy  
Apple achieved 100% renewable electricity for all corporate facilities—including offices, retail stores, and data centers—beginning in FY2018 [Apple_chunk_126]. In FY2024, Apple reported total electricity use of 3,777,000 MWh across corporate facilities, all sourced from renewables [Apple_chunk_125]. This includes a diversified portfolio of on- and off-site projects: wind (27%), solar (72%), microhydro (0.2%), and biogas fuel cells (0.2%), totaling 1,782 MW of operational renewable capacity globally [Apple_chunk_165]. Colocation facilities also achieved 100% renewable energy matching within the same country or regional grid since January 2018 [Apple_chunk_173]. Energy efficiency initiatives yielded cumulative electricity savings of 298,358 MWh/year in FY2024, alongside 124,034 MMBtu/year in fuel savings at corporate facilities [Apple_chunk_125].

#### ### GRI 303 Water  
In FY2024, Apple’s corporate facilities withdrew 1,756 million gallons of water globally, of which 1,532 million gallons were freshwater and 197 million gallons were recycled water [Apple_chunk_126]. Other alternative sources—including rainwater and recovered condensate—contributed 27 million gallons [Apple_chunk_127]. Apple defines “high-stress” water use based on the WRI Aqueduct Water Risk Atlas V4.0 and refines assessments with local knowledge [Apple_chunk_213]. The company reports participation in the Catawba-Wateree Water Management Group and certification efforts toward the Alliance for Water Stewardship (AWS) Standard for all Apple-owned data centers by 2025 [Apple_chunk_74][Apple_chunk_4]. Supplier water stewardship is advanced through the Supplier Clean Water program, targeting an average 50% water reuse rate by 2030 in high-stress basins [Apple_chunk_4].

#### ### GRI 305 Emissions  
Apple has been carbon neutral for corporate (Scope 1 + Scope 2) emissions since April 2020 and maintained this status through FY2023 via high-integrity carbon credits [Apple_chunk_118]. In FY2024, Apple’s corporate Scope 1 emissions totaled 55,200 metric tons CO₂e, and market-based Scope 2 emissions totaled 278,108 metric tons CO₂e across global corporate locations—including data centers, retail stores, and offices [Apple_chunk_166]. Notably, Scope 2 emissions from purchased electricity are zero; however, emissions from steam, heating, and cooling added 3,300 metric tons CO₂e in FY2024 [Apple_chunk_165]. Apple’s comprehensive carbon footprint—including Scope 3 categories such as product use (4.68 Mt CO₂e), manufacturing (29.03 Mt CO₂e), and other cloud services (953,200 t CO₂e, location-based)—was verified by third-party providers [Apple_chunk_181][Apple_chunk_183]. Apple’s net corporate emissions in FY2024 were 14.5 million metric tons CO₂e (gross: 15.3 Mt; offsets/removals: 0.7 Mt) [Apple_chunk_9].

#### ### GRI 306 Waste  
Apple’s corporate facilities achieved a 70% landfill diversion rate in FY2024—down from 74% in FY2023 [Apple_chunk_127]. Total waste disposed to landfill was 18,800 metric tonnes, while 36,500 metric tonnes were recycled and 4,100 metric tonnes composted [Apple_chunk_181]. Additional waste streams included 1,000 metric tonnes sent to waste-to-energy and 5,200 metric tonnes of construction & demolition (C&D) material landfilled [Apple_chunk_181]. Apple’s Zero Waste Program targets elimination of landfill-bound waste from corporate facilities and suppliers [Apple_chunk_4]. Supply chain waste diversion reached 600,000 metric tonnes in FY2024 [Apple_chunk_127].

#### ### TCFD Metrics & Targets  
Apple aligns its climate targets with a 1.5°C pathway and commits to carbon neutrality across its entire value chain by 2030—entailing a 75% reduction in gross emissions from the 2015 baseline (38.4 Mt CO₂e) [Apple_chunk_4][Apple_chunk_9]. FY2024 gross emissions stood at 15.3 Mt CO₂e, representing a 60% reduction versus 2015 [Apple_chunk_4]. Apple discloses Scope 1, 2, and selected Scope 3 emissions annually via CDP and its Environmental Progress Report [Apple_chunk_9]. Forward-looking metrics include supplier renewable electricity capacity (18.9 GW operational in FY2024) and progress toward requiring 100% renewable electricity for Apple production across the direct manufacturing supply chain by 2030 [Apple_chunk_126].

- **Scope 3 completeness**: While Business Travel, Employee Commute, Work-from-Home, and Other Cloud Services emissions are reported, emissions from *Fuel and Energy Related Activities*, *Waste*, and *End-of-Life Processing* are stated as “not verified by Apex” and lack quantitative disclosure [Apple_chunk_183].

### Social Performance Report: Apple Inc.  
*Based on Fiscal Year 2024 Disclosures (Reporting Period Ending September 2024)*  

#### ### GRI 403: Occupational Health & Safety  
Apple’s Environment, Health and Safety (EHS) Policy affirms its commitment to protecting the health and safety of employees, contractors, and suppliers globally [Apple_chunk_201]. The policy mandates that all employees receive EHS training in their primary language and requires suppliers to provide safe working conditions and treat workers with dignity and respect [Apple_chunk_201]. While Apple discloses its EHS management system is certified to ISO 14001:2015 for EMEA operations—including manufacturing, sales, delivery, and after-sales support—no quantitative occupational injury or illness metrics (e.g., TRIR, LTIR), incident rates, or near-miss reporting data are provided in the available context [Apple_chunk_202]. Similarly, no disclosures on occupational exposure monitoring, hazard-specific prevention programs (beyond chemical management), or worker participation mechanisms in safety governance are included.

#### ### GRI 404: Training  
Apple invests in supplier capacity-building through structured, scalable training initiatives. In fiscal year 2024, nearly 300 supplier facilities in China and Taiwan participated in Clean Energy Academies—customized workshops co-facilitated by renewable energy developers and industry experts—to advance clean energy procurement [Apple_chunk_34]. These academies supplement the Apple Clean Energy Portal, a resource hub offering country-specific guidance, training materials, and implementation tools for transitioning to 100% renewable electricity [Apple_chunk_34]. Internally, Apple develops “internal trainings and an engagement process for Apple employees and suppliers” to accelerate supplier clean energy adoption [Apple_chunk_34]. However, no data on employee training hours, participation rates, learning outcomes, or workforce development programs for Apple’s own 164,000 full-time equivalent employees (FY2024) are disclosed [Apple_chunk_129][Apple_chunk_128].

#### ### GRI 405: Diversity  
The provided context contains no disclosures related to workforce diversity—including gender, ethnicity, age, disability status, or inclusion metrics—for Apple’s global workforce of 164,000 full-time equivalent employees [Apple_chunk_129][Apple_chunk_128]. There are no reported diversity targets, representation statistics by level or function, pay equity analyses, or inclusive leadership initiatives. While Apple’s Supplier Code of Conduct is referenced as requiring suppliers to “treat workers with dignity and respect” and act “fairly and ethically,” no supplier-level diversity or inclusion assessments or requirements are described [Apple_chunk_201].

#### ### GRI 414: Supplier Social Assessment  
Apple’s Supplier Responsibility program is anchored in its Supplier Code of Conduct, which mandates ethical labor practices, safe working conditions, and fair treatment of workers [Apple_chunk_201]. The company engages suppliers through “deep supply chain engagement” initiatives, including Full Material Disclosure (FMD) and Chemical Safety Disclosure (CSD) programs, to assess chemical hazards and enforce stringent material safety standards [Apple_chunk_86]. Notably, Apple’s Regulated Substances Specification (RSS) — updated in March 2025 — codifies safer chemistry requirements exceeding local regulatory standards and includes new testing protocols for heavy metals in dyes [Apple_chunk_91]. In FY2024, Apple approved over 65 new safer cleaners, bringing the cumulative total to more than 200 approved for use across its supply chain [Apple_chunk_91]. However, the context provides no information on social audits, third-party verification of labor practices, remediation rates for nonconformities, worker voice mechanisms (e.g., grievance channels), or social performance scoring of suppliers. No data on supplier assessment coverage (e.g., % of Tier 1 suppliers audited), frequency, or findings related to forced labor, child labor, working hours, or living wages is disclosed.

### Governance Performance Overview  

Apple’s governance framework for environmental, social, and climate-related matters is anchored in formalized policies, third-party assured accountability mechanisms, and active participation in multi-stakeholder climate governance initiatives. The company demonstrates structured oversight of sustainability performance through board-level responsibility, robust internal controls, and independent external verification aligned with global standards including the GRI, TCFD, and ISO frameworks.  

### Board Oversight and Accountability Mechanisms  

Apple’s Board of Directors retains ultimate responsibility for enterprise-wide risk management and strategic sustainability objectives, including climate targets and ethical supply chain governance. While the specific composition or mandate of board committees is not disclosed in the provided context, management’s explicit accountability for assertions on green bond allocations—including selection of Eligibility Criteria and allocation of $1.1 billion in net proceeds to qualifying projects during the Reporting Period (September 29, 2019–September 28, 2024)—reflects a clear delegation of executive accountability under board supervision [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_159]. This assertion was subject to independent assurance by an external provider, confirming material fairness of the allocation claim [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_159].  

### Anti-Corruption and Ethical Conduct  

Apple maintains a Code of Ethics applicable across its assurance partners—such as Apex Companies, LLC—which explicitly prioritizes prevention of conflicts of interest and upholds high ethical standards in day-to-day operations [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_192]. Although Apple’s own corporate Code of Ethics is not excerpted in the context, its Supplier Code of Conduct is referenced as a binding instrument for vendor accountability, with resources publicly available at apple.com/supplychain [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_201]. No quantitative anti-corruption metrics (e.g., training coverage, incident reporting rates, or disciplinary actions) are disclosed.  

### Climate Governance and Risk Integration  

Apple embeds climate risk management into strategic decision-making through formal advocacy principles and cross-sectoral collaboration. Its Climate Advocacy Principles explicitly call for science-based targets aligned with the Paris Agreement, carbon pricing, fluorinated greenhouse gas (F-GHG) abatement policy in semiconductor and display manufacturing, and high-integrity corporate emissions disclosure using globally recognized standards [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_109]. These principles inform engagement with trade associations, where Apple assesses alignment with its values and works toward position harmonization [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_106]. Apple also participates in 11 formal climate governance coalitions—including RE100, America is All In, the Climate Group, and the MIT Climate & Sustainability Consortium—demonstrating institutional commitment to systemic policy change [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_106][Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_107].  

### Independent Assurance and Verification Practices  

Apple engages multiple independent third parties to verify environmental claims and data integrity. Apex Companies, LLC conducted assurance on supplier energy efficiency program data, concluding that Apple’s Energy, Water, Paper, and Scope 1/2/3 (Business Travel & Employee Commute) GHG emissions assertions are “materially correct” and fairly represented [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_183]. Trinity Consultants, Inc. performed a fiscal year 2024 (October 2023–September 2024) desktop review of F-GHG emissions and abatement performance across top semiconductor and display suppliers, verifying avoided emissions of 8,407,304 metric tons CO₂e [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_199]. Both firms affirmed independence, absence of conflict of interest, and adherence to quality assurance protocols (e.g., ISO 9001:2015 certification for Trinity) [Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_192][Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2025_chunk_199].
