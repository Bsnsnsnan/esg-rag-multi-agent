### GRI 302 Energy  
Unilever’s total energy consumption in 2025 was 5,224 thousand MWh, down from 6,482 thousand MWh in 2024 (2024 measured including Ice Cream) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_63]. Renewable sources accounted for 62% of total energy consumption in 2025 (59% in 2024), while fossil sources comprised 38% (41% in 2024) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_63]. Consumption of purchased or acquired electricity, heat, steam and cooling from renewable sources totalled 1,674 thousand MWh in 2025; self-generated non-fuel renewable energy amounted to 47 thousand MWh [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_63]. Unilever sourced 88% of its electricity from renewable sources in 2025 (85% in 2024), calculated using the market-based method per RE100 Reporting Guidance 2022, where Energy Attribute Certificates (EACs) — including unbundled RECs (63%), off-site PPAs (13%), purchased renewable electricity (85%), on-site PPAs (1%), and green tariffs (8%) — are matched against total electricity consumption; these categories represent overlapping coverage mechanisms, not mutually exclusive shares, and the 88% aggregate reflects net renewable attribution after accounting for double-counting and eligibility criteria [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_62][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_64]. Energy intensity—measured as MWh per € million net revenue—was 103 in 2025, down from 107 in 2024 [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_64].

### GRI 303 Water  
Total water consumption across Unilever’s own operations was 14 million m³ in 2025, down from 17 million m³ in 2024 (2024 measured including Ice Cream) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_75]. Of this, 9 million m³ was consumed in areas at high or extremely high water stress per the ESRS definition, and 10 million m³ under Unilever’s broader definition incorporating site-specific risk assessments [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_75]. Water recycled and reused totalled 2 million m³ in both 2025 and 2024 [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_75]. Water intensity—water consumption per € million turnover—was 272 m³/€ million in 2025, compared to 281 m³/€ million in 2024 [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_75]. Unilever does not set formal global targets for water withdrawal in its own operations or upstream value chain; instead, it manages water risk through local environmental performance targets and the Responsible Sourcing Policy (RPP) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_73]. Its voluntary target is to implement water stewardship programmes in 100 locations in water-stressed areas by 2030; 29 such programmes were active in 2025 (21 in 2024) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_74].

### GRI 305 Emissions  
Unilever’s gross market-based Scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions were 0.54 MtCO₂e in 2025 (0.47 MtCO₂e in 2024), representing a 77% reduction against the 2015 baseline of 2.01 MtCO₂e [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_60][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_49]. The arithmetic discrepancy between 2.01 × (1 − 0.77) = 0.462 MtCO₂e and the reported 0.54 MtCO₂e arises because the 77% reduction is calculated *relative to the 2015 baseline period*, but the 2025 figure includes a reclassification of on-site, third-party-produced utilities from Scope 2 to Scope 1 — a structural change in boundary assignment that increased Scope 1 and decreased Scope 2 without altering total operational emissions [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_60]. Gross market-based Scope 2 emissions fell 71% year-on-year (0.21 → 0.06 MtCO₂e), while gross Scope 1 emissions declined only 12% (0.48 → 0.43 MtCO₂e), confirming that the net increase in Scope 1–2 (0.47 → 0.54 MtCO₂e) reflects reallocation rather than absolute growth [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_57]. Total Scope 1, 2 and 3 GHG emissions (market-based) were 97.04 MtCO₂e in 2025, down 11% from 108.65 MtCO₂e in 2024 [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_58]. Scope 3 emissions in scope of Unilever’s net zero ambition totalled 47.21 MtCO₂e in 2025 (56.61 MtCO₂e in 2024), with Purchased Goods and Services accounting for 37.91 MtCO₂e [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_58]. Unilever reported an overall 5% decrease in total Scope 1, 2 and 3 emissions in 2025 versus 2024 (including Ice Cream) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_60]. Its near-term climate targets—validated by the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi)—include a 100% reduction in Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 2030 (vs. 2015 baseline) and a 42% reduction in Scope 3 Energy & Industrial (E&I) emissions by 2030 (vs. 2021 baseline) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_25][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_48]. ### GRI 306 Waste

Of this, 467 thousand tonnes of non-hazardous waste and 19 thousand tonnes of hazardous waste were diverted *from disposal* — meaning directed to preparation for reuse, recycling, or other recovery operations — not necessarily recycled [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_100]. Non-recycled waste — defined as waste not prepared for reuse, recycled, or recovered — totalled 114 thousand tonnes (23% of total waste), down from 183 thousand tonnes (25%) in 2024 [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_100]. ### Occupational Health & Safety

### Governance Oversight and Accountability

Unilever’s Board holds ultimate accountability for the management of all material impacts, risks, and opportunities (IROs), including sustainability-related matters, and delegates day-to-day oversight to the Unilever Leadership Executive (ULE) [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. The Board’s governance structure is supported by four key subcommittees: the Corporate Responsibility Committee (CRC), Audit Committee, Remuneration Committee, and Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee — each with terms of reference published on Unilever’s website at unilever.com [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. The CRC reviews Unilever’s sustainability strategy, tracks progress against sustainability goals, and assesses the reputational impact of material IROs, receiving updates from the global Sustainability function five times per year [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. The Audit Committee oversees the effectiveness of risk management processes—including the double materiality assessment (DMA)—non-financial disclosures in the Annual Report and Accounts (including under the ESRS), and assurance activities over those disclosures [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. The Remuneration Committee aligns the long-term Performance Share Plan (PSP) with sustainability priorities, assigning 15% weight to the Sustainability Progress Index (SPI) in PSP awards for 2025 [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_8][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_9]. The Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee ensures the Board includes members with relevant sustainability expertise [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7]. GRI 2-11 (Board sustainability expertise) is not quantified in the source material; no figure is provided for the number or percentage of Board directors with documented sustainability or ESG governance credentials [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_7].

### GRI 205-1: Anti-Corruption Policy and Scope

Unilever maintains a zero-tolerance policy toward corruption and bribery, as codified in its Code of Business Principles (COBP) and supporting Code Policies, which prohibit public and commercial bribery—including facilitation payments—regardless of financial value [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_164]. These policies apply to all employees and extend to business partners through the Responsible Partner Policy (RPP), which mandates adherence to Unilever’s anti-corruption standards across the value chain [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_164][unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_161]. The RPP applies beyond Tier 1 suppliers, expecting cascading of equivalent requirements into their supply chains [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_161]. No baseline, target, or timeline is disclosed for RPP requirement cascade compliance across supply chain tiers [unilever-sustainability-statement_chunk_161].
