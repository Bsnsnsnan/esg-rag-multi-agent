### GRI 302 Energy  
Amazon reported 131,000 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of annual carbon-free energy production in 2025 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_22]. Carbon-free energy includes electricity generated from wind, solar, nuclear, hydroelectric, and geothermal sources, as well as site energy contracts and green tariffs that add new renewable projects to the grid [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_14]. As of January 2026, Amazon’s renewable energy portfolio comprised 375 utility-scale projects globally—264 solar, 109 wind, and two nuclear—with a total expected capacity of 41,670 MW across 30 U.S. states and 15 European countries [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_114][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_22]. Emissions from purchased electricity (Scope 2) totaled 3.74 million metric tons of CO₂e (MMTCO₂e) in 2025, representing 4.6% of Amazon’s total carbon footprint of 80.85 MMTCO₂e [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_112]. This growth was driven by electricity use in data centers, electrification of the delivery network, and building electrification [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_19]. **The context does not disclose location-based or market-based Scope 2 emissions separately, nor does it specify whether the 131,000 GWh refers to physical generation, contractual procurement of renewable energy attributes, or a hybrid** [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_22].

### GRI 303 Water  
Amazon aims to be water positive—returning more water to communities than it uses—across global direct data center operations by 2030 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33]. The baseline year for this target is not disclosed in the context, nor is an absolute volume target (e.g., liters replenished annually) specified [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33]. In 2025, Amazon’s leased, owned, and shared data centers withdrew 9.4 billion liters of water [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33]. The company achieved 75% progress toward its 2030 data center water positive goal, up from 53% in 2024 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33]. Global Water Use Effectiveness (WUE) improved to 0.12 liters per kilowatt-hour (L/kWh) in 2025—a 20% reduction from 0.15 L/kWh in 2024 and a 52% reduction from 0.25 L/kWh in 2021 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_35]. In India, Amazon achieved 120% water positivity in 2025—two years ahead of its 2027 goal—through on-site sewage treatment plants (28 buildings, yielding ~298 million liters/year), rainwater harvesting (~178 million liters infiltrated in 2025), and expanded replenishment investments [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_37]. Amazon invested in 45 global water replenishment projects, returning 9.4 billion liters to communities by end-2025 and contracting for over 18 billion liters of annual replenishment volume in future years [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_37]. **The context does not specify water withdrawal by source (e.g., surface water, groundwater, municipal supply) or identify root causes for the remaining gap to full water positivity** [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_34].

### GRI 305 Emissions  
Amazon’s total carbon footprint (Scopes 1, 2, and 3) was 80.85 million metric tons of CO₂e (MMTCO₂e) in 2025, up 16% from 69.55 MMTCO₂e in 2024 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_5][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_112]. Scope 1 emissions were 15.37 MMTCO₂e; Scope 2, 3.74 MMTCO₂e; and Scope 3, 61.74 MMTCO₂e [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_112]. Environmental Attribute Certificates (EACs) delivered a net reduction of 199.4 thousand metric tons of CO₂e (KMT CO₂e) across Scopes 1, 3.1, 3.3, and 3.4—including 41.2 KMT CO₂e reduction in Scope 1 via Sustainable Aviation Fuel (SAF) and Renewable Diesel certificates [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_15][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_113]. Carbon intensity—measured as grams CO₂e per dollar of revenue—was 112.8 gCO₂e/$Revenue in 2025 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_5][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_112]. **The context discloses only partial Scope 3 category breakdowns: Scopes 3.1 (purchased goods and services), 3.3 (fuel- and energy-related activities), and 3.4 (downstream transportation and distribution) are quantified, but emissions for the remaining 12 GHG Protocol Scope 3 categories (e.g., Category 1 — upstream transportation, Category 11 — use of sold products beyond devices, Category 15 — investments) are not provided, nor is a GRI-compliant rationale for exclusions** [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_112][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_113].

### GRI 306 Waste  
In 2025, Amazon diverted 84% of its operational waste from landfill and incineration without energy recovery—82% through recycling or composting and 2% via incineration with energy recovery [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_47]. Waste per shipped unit decreased by 5% compared to 2024 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_47][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_41]. Cardboard and wood constituted 64% of Amazon’s operational waste footprint, with nearly 100% recycled [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_47]. Across data centers, Amazon avoided over one million hard drive purchases since 2023 and prevented 225,000 metric tons of CO₂e through hardware reuse and resale since 2020 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_45][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_41]. For Devices, 89 supplier sites achieved a verified 99% waste diversion rate in 2025, including 6% via thermal processing with energy recovery [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_45]. **The context does not disclose waste composition beyond cardboard and wood, nor does it explain reasons for residual landfill disposal (e.g., contamination, local infrastructure limits), nor state whether waste data are assured** [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_47][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_45].

### TCFD Strategy, Risk Management, Metrics & Targets  
Amazon’s climate strategy is anchored in The Climate Pledge and structured around five pillars: supply chain decarbonization, direct operations decarbonization, emissions reduction from purchased electricity, carbon neutralization, and collective action [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_10]. Risk management integrates AI-driven supplier risk identification and Higg Facility Environmental Module (Higg FEM) assessments to prioritize audits and verify performance on carbon, waste, and water [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_13][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_10]. Key metrics include carbon intensity (gCO₂e/$Revenue), WUE (L/kWh), and waste diversion rate (%), all tracked annually against 2040 net-zero, 2030 water-positive, and 2030 zero-waste-to-landfill targets [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_5][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_33][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_45]. **The context does not describe any TCFD-aligned scenario analysis—including assumptions, scenarios tested (e.g., 1.5°C, 2°C), or strategic implications—or disclose physical climate risk assessment methodology** [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_10].

### Occupational Health & Safety  
Amazon’s 2025 Global Recordable Incident Rate (RIR) improved 14% year over year; its Global Lost Time Incident Rate (LTIR) also improved 14% year over year [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76]. Over the six-year period 2019–2025, RIR improved by 43% and LTIR by 70% globally [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_78]. In the U.S., under the General Warehousing and Storage industry classification, RIR improved 16% year over year (2024–2025) and 39% since 2019; LTIR was flat year over year but improved 79% since 2019 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76]. Under the Courier and Express Delivery Services classification, RIR improved 19% year over year and 59% since 2019; LTIR improved 28% year over year and 81% since 2019 [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76]. Amazon employed more than 11,000 safety professionals worldwide in 2025 and conducted 10.4 million global safety inspections — a 33% increase from 2024 — to validate engineering controls and inform iterative improvements [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_77][2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76]. The source does not disclose absolute RIR or LTIR values for 2019, 2024, or 2025, nor does it specify the denominator (e.g., hours worked), normalization basis (e.g., per 100,000 hours), or OSHA alignment of definitions [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76]. No science-aligned, time-bound target (e.g., “RIR ≤ 2.0 by 2030”) is stated, and no explanation is provided for why U.S. LTIR was flat year over year despite concurrent RIR improvement [2025-amazon-sustainability-report_chunk_76].

### Training
