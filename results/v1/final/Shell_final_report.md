## Environmental

### GRI 302 Energy  
Shell’s total energy consumption in 2025 was 269 million MWh, down from 289 million MWh in 2024, primarily due to portfolio changes and greenhouse gas (GHG) abatement and transformation projects [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_829]. Of the energy consumed by facilities under Shell’s operational control (189 million MWh in 2025), 99% came from fossil sources (187 million MWh), comprising crude oil and petroleum products (40 million MWh), natural gas (108 million MWh), other fossil sources (10 million MWh), and purchased or acquired electricity, heat, steam and cooling from fossil sources (28 million MWh) [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_828]. Renewable energy consumption under operational control was 2.6 million MWh in 2025 — including 2.4 million MWh from purchased or acquired electricity, heat, steam and cooling from renewable sources, 0.03 million MWh from fuels from renewable sources (e.g., biomass, biofuels, biogas, hydrogen from renewable sources), and 0.1 million MWh from self-generated non-fuel renewable energy [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_829]. Energy intensity per net revenue remained stable at 0.001 MWh/$ in both 2024 and 2025, though Shell notes this metric is not considered reliable for cross-entity or longitudinal comparison due to boundary misalignment between energy consumption (ESRS reporting boundary) and net revenue (financial control boundary) [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_828].

### GRI 303 Water  
Water consumption reported against Shell’s operational control boundary was 86 million cubic metres in 2025, down from 90 million cubic metres in 2024 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_878]. Of this, approximately 72 million cubic metres (84%) was used for manufacturing oil products and chemicals, and 13 million cubic metres for oil and gas production [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_870]. In 2025, Shell consumed 8 million cubic metres of water in areas of high water stress, a reduction from 21 million cubic metres in 2024 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_985]. Water recycled and reused amounted to 25 million cubic metres in 2025, up from 23 million cubic metres in 2024 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_985]. Shell retired its prior commitment to reduce fresh water consumption in water-stressed areas, having met it ahead of schedule in 2022; the commitment formally ended in 2025 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_876].

### GRI 305 Emissions  
Shell’s gross Scope 1 and 2 GHG emissions (net of carbon credits) were 53 million tonnes CO₂e in 2025, down from 58 million tonnes CO₂e in 2024 and 83 million tonnes CO₂e in the 2016 baseline year [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_204][shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_1098]. This reflects a 36% reduction since 2016 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_29]. The 2025 reduction was driven by divestments (4.8 million tonnes), emission-reduction activities and purchase of renewable electricity (1.2 million tonnes), and changes in output (0.1 million tonnes), partly offset by acquisitions (1.4 million tonnes) [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_1098]. Shell’s methane emissions intensity for operated oil and gas assets with marketed gas was 0.04% in 2025, well below its target of <0.2% and consistent with its ambition to achieve near-zero intensity by 2030 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_29][shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_1119]. Routine flaring from upstream operations was eliminated in January 2025, independent of the March 2025 sale of SPDC [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_29][shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_165].

### Occupational Health & Safety  
In 2025, Shell reported 4 fatalities among contractor colleagues in Shell-operated ventures — one in Malaysia (EcoOils facility), one in Malaysia (mobile drilling rig), one in Argentina (road traffic incident), and one in the North Sea (fall from height) — with no employee fatalities [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_973]. FPI is defined by Shell as “the outcome of a work-related injury from which the worker cannot or is not expected to return to their previous pre-incident whole person function as a result of an acute, single incident”, consistent with IOGP metrics [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_974]. The FPI frequency (FPI-F) increased to 2.1 cases per 100 million working hours in 2025, up from 1.7 in 2024 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_974][shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_973]. These metrics cover employees and contractors under Mode 1 (Shell-controlled workplace) and Mode 2 (shared interface management) contractual arrangements; Mode 3 contractors are excluded unless working on Shell premises [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_975]. The source material does not disclose total working hours for 2024 or 2025, nor does it state whether the rise in LTIF/TRCF reflects operational changes, reporting refinements, or emerging risks [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_973]. ### Governance Structure and Board Oversight  
Shell plc’s governance framework is structured around the Board of Directors and the Executive Committee (EC), with the Board comprising 12 members—including two executive and 10 non-executive directors—as at December 31, 2025 (2024: 12 members, 2 executive and 10 non-executive members) [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_757]. All non-executive directors are considered independent in character and judgement, representing 83% of the Board as at December 31, 2025 (2024: 83%) [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_757]. The Board maintains primary oversight of Shell’s strategy—including its Energy Transition Strategy 2024, endorsed in March 2024—and monitors performance against longer-term business targets, including climate-related ambitions [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_759]. Sustainability matters, including climate-related impacts, risks and opportunities, are reviewed and addressed on an integrated basis alongside other business issues throughout the year [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_759]. The Board delegates authority for operational management to the CEO, who chairs the EC and is supported by EC members in implementing strategy and monitoring progress [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_242]. This structure aligns with GRI 2-11 (governance bodies’ roles and responsibilities) and TCFD Governance (g1–g4), as the Board explicitly oversees strategy, risk, and sustainability integration across time horizons defined as short term (one year), medium term (2–10 years), and long term (beyond 10 years) [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_754].

### Board Diversity, Composition and Succession  
Shell’s Board Diversity Policy—first published in March 2024—commits to gender balance, with women constituting 42% of the Board as at December 31, 2025 (2024: 42%), corresponding to a female-to-male ratio of 0.7 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_757]. The Policy sets a target to maintain representation of both men and women at or above 40%, and aims to have at least one senior Board position (Chair, CEO, Senior Independent Director or CFO) held by a woman [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_376]. The source material does not disclose the baseline year for this senior-position target, nor whether it has been achieved as of December 31, 2025 [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_376]. Shell also aims to maintain or exceed having at least one Board member from a minority ethnic background [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_376]; however, the absolute number and percentage of Board members from minority ethnic backgrounds for 2025 and 2024 are not disclosed [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_376]. The Nomination and Succession Committee (NOMCO) oversees development of a diverse pipeline for Board succession and ensures all appointments follow a formal, rigorous and transparent procedure based on merit and objective criteria, with due regard for diversity factors [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_379]. The 2025 Board Performance Review was externally facilitated by Jan Hall of No. 4 through one-on-one interviews with all Board members, the Company Secretary, External Audit partner, and three EC members, supplemented by observation of Board and Committee meetings and review of pre-meeting materials [shell-integrated-annual-and-sustainability-report_chunk_370].
